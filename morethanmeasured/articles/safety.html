Safety

Building a family safety plan

More Than Measured · September 2026 · 2 min read

Safety planning works best before an emergency. The useful question is not “what product should we buy?” but “what could happen, what information would someone need, and what layers reduce the risk?”

Identify the situations that worry you

Consider wandering, bolting in parking lots, water access, opening doors, climbing, getting separated in public, inability to communicate identifying information or difficulty responding to emergency instructions. Different risks need different plans.

Use layers

No lock, alarm, wearable or identification tag should be treated as a complete safety system. Combine appropriate supervision, environmental safeguards, identification, communication planning and caregiver procedures.

Prepare a quick information sheet

Keep a current photo, identifying information, emergency contacts, communication information, important medical details and likely destinations or attractions where appropriate. Make sure another caregiver knows where it is.

Plan the handoff

Babysitters, relatives, school staff and other caregivers need concise information about risks and what to do. A plan hidden in one person's phone is not useful if that person is unavailable.

Review after changes

New skills can change risk. A child who learns to operate a deadbolt, open a gate or move a chair may make yesterday's setup obsolete. Revisit the plan as abilities and environments change.

Emergency note: use emergency services when there is immediate danger. Product recommendations on MTM are not substitutes for supervision, individualized professional guidance, local emergency planning or required safety equipment.

Take what fits. Start small. Adjust along the way.
