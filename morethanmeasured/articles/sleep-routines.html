Sleep

Building a calmer bedtime routine

More Than Measured · September 2026 · 2 min read

Bedtime often goes better when the child can predict what happens next. The goal is not a perfect routine. It is a repeatable sequence that reduces uncertainty and gives caregivers useful clues when sleep changes.

Start with a few anchors

A long checklist can become another source of stress. Pick a few steps you can repeat most nights, such as bathroom, pajamas, a drink, one quiet activity and lights down. Keep the order similar. If pictures are easier to follow than spoken reminders, use a short visual sequence.

Use the environment as a cue

Lowering activity, light and noise can help signal that the day is changing. Some children prefer steady background sound while others need a quieter room. Clothing, bedding texture and room temperature can matter too. Change one thing at a time when possible so you can tell whether it actually helped.

Track patterns without turning sleep into homework

Record bedtime, approximate time asleep, unusual waking, naps and anything notably different that day. A simple note like “grandma day,” “late nap,” or “very active evening” can be more useful than pages of detail. Look for repeated patterns over several days rather than treating one difficult night as proof of a cause.

When the routine falls apart

Keep a shortened version available. On a late or difficult night, two familiar steps may be better than forcing the full routine. Returning to the normal sequence the next day helps preserve the cue without making bedtime a contest.

Choosing products

Before buying a sleep product, identify the problem you are trying to solve. A visual timer addresses a different need than blackout curtains or a sound machine. Avoid assuming that a product marketed as “sensory” or “autism friendly” is automatically a good fit. For weighted or other specialized sleep products, discuss safety and suitability with an appropriate healthcare professional.

Remember: persistent sleep problems, breathing concerns, pain, unusual movements or major changes in sleep deserve discussion with a healthcare professional. This guide is educational and is not medical advice.

Take what fits. Start small. Adjust along the way.
