Preparing for outings and travel

More Than Measured Resource Library · September 2026

A successful outing does not have to mean staying until everyone else is ready to leave. Preparation should give the family more options, including an easy way to change the plan.

Preview what can be predicted

Show where you are going, how you will get there, what will probably happen first and when you expect to leave. Photos, a simple written plan or a short verbal preview may be enough.

Pack the familiar

A small outing kit can include communication supports, a familiar regulation item, hearing protection if used, snacks, water, wipes, spare clothing, chargers and anything needed for toileting. The best kit is the one you will actually carry.

Identify a lower-stimulation option

Before arriving, look for a quiet corner, outdoor space, vehicle access or other place where the family can reduce demands. Knowing the exit route before stress rises makes it easier to use.

Plan for waiting

Lines and delays are often harder than the main activity. Bring one or two reliable waiting activities and communicate what the wait is for. If possible, investigate timed entry, accessibility services or quieter hours directly with the venue.

Debrief briefly

Afterward, record what helped and what you wish you had packed. That turns each outing into information for the next one without requiring a complicated travel journal.

Useful standard: leaving early, taking a break or changing plans can be a successful use of the plan. The purpose is participation that works for the family, not endurance for its own sake.
