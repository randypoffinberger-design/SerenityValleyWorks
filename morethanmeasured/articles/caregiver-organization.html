Caregivers

Organizing the information everyone needs

More Than Measured · September 2026 · 2 min read

A family can know a child's routines so well that important details become invisible. The test of an organization system is whether another trusted caregiver can find the right information when the usual caregiver is unavailable.

Separate essential from interesting

Emergency contacts, allergies, medications, safety concerns, communication needs and critical routines should be easy to find. Longer notes and history can live elsewhere. Do not bury urgent information in ten pages of background.

Write routines for someone who does not already know them

“Normal bedtime” means nothing to a new caregiver. A short sequence with approximate times, preferred comfort items and what usually happens when the routine is disrupted is far more useful.

Include what helps during distress

List known triggers when appropriate, early signs of overload, useful communication, calming options and things that tend to make the situation worse. Keep the language practical and respectful.

Maintain an offline backup

Digital organization is convenient, but batteries die and connections fail. A concise printed sheet or binder can provide essential information during outages, travel or device problems. Store sensitive information securely.

Review instead of endlessly adding

Old information can be worse than missing information. Schedule occasional reviews to remove outdated contacts, doses, routines and preferences. Date important pages so another caregiver knows when they were last checked.

Why this matters to MTM: one of the app's goals is to turn scattered family knowledge into information that can be used when it matters, without making caregivers recreate the same story over and over.

Take what fits. Start small. Adjust along the way.
