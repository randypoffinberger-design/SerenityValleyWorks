PLAY & LEARNING

Using interests to support play and learning

More Than Measured Resource Library · September 2026

A strong interest can be a doorway into connection. Instead of treating the interest as something to work around, start there and gently expand what can happen around it.

Join before directing

Watch how the child uses the toy, object or topic. Comment, imitate or participate without immediately changing the activity. Shared attention can grow from being genuinely interested in what already matters to them.

Add one small variation

If cars are the interest, add a ramp, a garage, colors, counting or a simple turn-taking game. If letters are the interest, use them in scavenger hunts, names or matching. Keep the familiar part visible while adding something new.

There is more than one way to play

Repeatedly lining up, sorting or examining parts can still be meaningful engagement. You can model another option without requiring the child to abandon their preferred way of interacting.

Choose toys with room to grow

Open-ended materials can often be used in several ways and across developmental stages. But a highly specific favorite can be just as valuable if it reliably creates connection or motivation. Cost and complexity do not determine play value.

Watch for genuine engagement

Track what the child returns to, shares with another person, tolerates for longer or begins using in a new way. Those observations can reveal growth that a traditional milestone list misses.

Product filter: ask whether an item fits the child's interests and abilities, can be used safely, and adds something the household does not already have. “Educational” on a package is not enough by itself.
